# Toolbox Talk — Working Safely at Height

**Delivered by:** Supervisor · **Duration:** about 5 minutes · **Crew:** site crew

## Why we're talking about this

Falls from height remain one of the leading causes of serious injury and death in our
industry, year after year. Whether you are on a ladder, a scaffold, a roof or a platform,
the ground does not forgive a mistake. Safety is a mindset, and it starts before you climb.
Every one of us has a family that expects us home tonight, and every one of us has a part
to play in making sure that happens. So take the next few minutes seriously — this applies
to everyone on site, whatever you're working on today.

## The risks of working at height

Working at height is dangerous in many ways. Ladders can slip if they are not footed or
tied. Scaffolds can be misused or overloaded. Roofs can be fragile, and weather can turn
quickly — wind, rain and ice all make surfaces treacherous. Tools and materials can fall
and strike people below. Complacency is the biggest danger of all: most falls happen to
experienced people doing routine work, because familiarity breeds shortcuts. Statistics
show that the majority of serious falls happen from relatively low heights, which proves
that no height is a safe height. People get hurt doing this kind of work every year across
the industry — don't let it be you.

## Staying safe up there

- Always wear your harness and clip on where required — your harness is your lifeline.
- Hard hats on at all times; chin straps done up when working aloft.
- Maintain three points of contact on ladders, and never overreach.
- Inspect your equipment before use and report any defects to your supervisor.
- Keep your work area tidy — good housekeeping prevents slips and dropped items.
- Follow the site rules and the method statement at all times.
- If in doubt, stop and ask — there is always time to do it safely.

Remember: rules are written in someone else's bad day. Following them is how we all stay
safe. Your PPE only works if you wear it properly, so check it, wear it, and look after it.

Any questions? Good — let's get out there and have a safe shift.

## Three things to take away

- Stay alert — complacency causes falls.
- Use your PPE properly, every time.
- Look out for each other and report anything unsafe.
