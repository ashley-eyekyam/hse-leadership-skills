## 1. Node definition and design intent

Node 4 covers the reactor feed line from the feed surge drum to the reactor inlet,
including the feed pump, control valve and associated pipework. The design intent is
continuous forward flow of crude feed at normal operating pressure and temperature to
the reactor inlet.

## 2. Deviation worksheet

| Deviation | Cause | Consequence | Safeguards |
|---|---|---|---|
| High pressure | Blocked outlet downstream of the control valve | Overpressure of the feed line and potential loss of containment | Relief valve; high-pressure alarm; operating procedure |
| Low flow | Feed pump degradation or suction restriction | Reduced feed to the reactor; potential reactor upset | Low-flow alarm; operator response |
| No flow | Feed pump trip; control valve fails closed | Loss of feed; reactor thermal excursion risk | Pump trip alarm; standby pump; procedure |
| Reverse flow | Pump stop with downstream pressure higher than suction | Backflow toward the surge drum | Check valve on pump discharge |
| High temperature | Preheater control failure | Feed above design temperature at the reactor inlet | Temperature indication and alarm |
| Contamination (as well as) | Water carry-over from the surge drum | Slug of water to the hot reactor feed circuit | Drum level control; low-point drain routine |

## 3. Study narrative

The worksheet above records the deviations examined for Node 4, developed line by line
from the node's design intent. Each deviation was traced from its credible causes through
to its process consequences, and the safeguards currently provided by the design and by
site operating practice were recorded against each line.

The study concluded that the node's protection is provided principally by the relief
valve, the alarm suite and the operating procedures listed, and the worksheet has been
issued to the project file as the record of the Node 4 examination.
